Magnetic Inspection Crawler (Capstone, In Progress)

For our MECH 45X capstone, our team of six is designing a robotic crawler for DarkVision Technologies. It will carry their Gen 3 ultrasound imaging heads across steel infrastructure for demos and trade shows.

01

Goals & requirements

The crawler needs to carry the imaging heads across steel pipes and flat plates in horizontal, vertical and inverted orientations, while holding the heads at a constant height off the surface. We broke this down into four main functions: attach to the structure, traverse it, control the imaging head standoff, and accept user control.

Pass/fail requirements

We built the requirements from DarkVision's project brief, our meetings with their engineering team, and our own research. Every concept has to meet all of them before it gets scored. The key requirements and targets are:

RequirementTarget
Traverse ferrous steel (pipe and plate) in 2 axesAxial and circumferential motion on steel surfaces down to a 230mm radius
Operate in any orientationNo slip or detachment at full payload
Fail safe on power loss, with an e-stopNo detachment when power is cut
Cross discontinuities (rivets, seams)Obstacles up to 6mm high
Carry 1–4 imaging heads with adjustable standoff10–50mm standoff, held to ±0.1mm once set
Positional accuracy100µm over 20mm of travel

02

Evaluation criteria

Concepts that pass every requirement are compared with a weighted value equation built from seven evaluation criteria:

Value = 0.25x₁ + 0.05x₂ + 0.20x₃ + 0.15x₄ + 0.15x₅ + 0.15x₆ + 0.05x₇

Each concept's estimate for a criterion is converted to a score from 0 to 1 using that criterion's evaluation curve, and the scores are then combined with the weights below. Concepts that fail any pass/fail requirement aren't scored.

CriterionMetricWeight
x₁ Constant standoff over curvature±0.1mm on a 230mm curvature radius25%
x₂ Traverse speed50mm/s proposed, higher is better5%
x₃ Positional accuracy100µm over 20mm of travel20%
x₄ Friction toleranceWorks at µ ≥ 0.2 on 1018 brushed steel15%
x₅ Design simplicityPart count, assembly complexity, off-the-shelf vs custom15%
x₆ Path efficiencyImaging time / (traverse + imaging time)15%
x₇ BOM cost$5,000 or lower5%
03

Concepts

We sketched three concepts and compared them against our weighted evaluation criteria. Constant standoff over curvature and positional accuracy carry the most weight.

Concept A: Pivot

Large magnetic wheels to roll over obstacles and a spine pivot with passive roll/pitch and active yaw. Each wheel has a non-backdrivable worm gearbox, so the crawler holds position when power is cut. The imaging heads sit on a lever arm mount, and height is set with shims. It has a robust drivetrain and simple steering, but head height is manual and there's no active obstacle compensation.

Concept B: Crab

Every wheel is its own steerable drive module, so the crawler can move axially and circumferentially on a pipe without turning its body. The imaging heads sit on a central mount with linear suspension. It's symmetric and simple to steer, but scanning coverage and imaging head configurations are more limited.

Concept C: Tank

Magnet-embedded treads give the largest contact area and the highest attraction force to the surface. The imaging sled has actuated height control, and the wheels have independent suspension. It offers the most electronic control over the imaging head, but it's the most complex to manufacture, and the treads risk detaching.
